# Neighbourhoods

Routes, obstacles and how they occupy the road. The [Tuning page](https://archie.tristamittermayer.com/tuning/) shows the current game data.

## Forks

- **Forks:** the road meets a cross street; after neighbourhoods 1 and 2 a fork card offers three paths (left, straight, right), each leading to a neighbourhood you haven't walked this walk other than Yonge Street, sorted easiest on the left to hardest on the right, ties in table order. The fork after neighbourhood 3 leads only to Yonge Street, so it shows one path. Each path card shows the neighbourhood's name and difficulty, and the upgrade card it carries: the upgrade's name over a few words on what it does, said in full on the enlarged card. At a fork with more than one path, [one path carries no upgrade](encounters.md#upgrade-cards). Its card says "No upgrade" with no plaque; Yonge Street's path after neighbourhood 3 always carries one. The fork card's subtitle reads "Most paths bring an upgrade". Picking a path picks the next neighbourhood and its upgrade, if any, at once. An End walk button under the paths ends the walk there (see [End walk](rules.md#end-walk)). After a path is chosen, the walk holds with the new neighbourhood's banner showing until its road is ready, then walks on.

## Roster

Normal walks visit 4 of the 6 neighbourhoods with no repeats. [Cheats](cheats.md#cheats) can revisit a neighbourhood or restart the current one.

Cabbagetown, The Beaches, the Distillery District and Yonge Street each also have their own card obstacle (see [Encounters and debuffs](encounters.md#encounters-and-debuffs)), and every neighbourhood has a few obstacles and movers found nowhere else; no mover or plain obstacle but the squirrel appears in more than 3 neighbourhoods, each street cart look counting as its own.

The [neighbourhood variety targets](tuning.md#targets) guide roster composition. A moving and a parked car share one visual family, and distinct street cart looks count separately. The Distillery District has 24 visual families, 7 fixed kinds and 17 moving kinds. The planned mover share rises from about 50% in High Park through 52% in Cabbagetown, 54% in Kensington Market, 56% in The Beaches and 58% in the Distillery District to 60% on Yonge Street. Fork position can shift those shares slightly. At the same walk position, total obstacle requests increase across that same difficulty order.

The street cart, a 2-lane obstacle that never moves, stands in every neighbourhood with that neighbourhood's look: flowers in Cabbagetown, fruit and veg in Kensington Market, hot dogs in High Park and Kensington Market, ice cream in the Distillery District and The Beaches, and newspapers and magazines on Yonge Street. Where a neighbourhood has two looks, each cart picks one.

## Spawning

- Spawning always leaves a reachable path, accounting for every mover's moves, so every hit is avoidable (see [Spawning details](#spawning-details))
- Scroll speed and obstacle density rise gently each neighbourhood
- Obstacles span lanes and rows (shapes in the data). None is wider than 3 lanes, every row leaves at least 2 lanes open, and obstacle rows leave room for the pack's full length.
- The Distillery District has fixed obstacles 1 and 2 lanes wide. Every other neighbourhood has fixed obstacles of all three widths: at least one 1 lane wide, one 2 lanes wide and one 3 lanes wide
- Obstacles, fixed and moving, go toward the lanes left clear longest, so no lane, the sides included, stays clear for long
- The first neighbourhood's first obstacle reaches the pack about two seconds in at the soonest, so a walk never opens with a hit

## Moving hazards

Each obstacle uses its neighbourhood look, with any of its local skins equally likely. Some kinds can move (each kind's movement is in the data), and every one that can always moves. Art stays upright and readable while its position follows its path; a turn does not rotate its billboard into the road.

- **Cues:** a mover first warns before moving: its first move is telegraphed about 0.6 seconds ahead, and its later moves come with no cue of their own unless its movement below says otherwise, a stepper's and a hopper's each after a short random pause once the move before ends. A darting animal shows as soon as its stretch of road comes into view (at the road's top edge, if it sets off farther up), waiting at its edge until its cue. An in-lane mover's cue shows as soon as its stretch of road comes into view, so it can last longer than 0.6 seconds
- **Hits and pickups:** a mover may pass over a tip or a treat, which stays where it is, and may end a move on a tip, but never on a treat or a lucky find, so those can always be grabbed without a hit. A mover's hit is its standing obstacle's hit
- **Only ahead of the pack:** a mover moves only while it is still ahead of the pack: once it reaches the pack's rows it makes no more moves, and nothing ever moves back up the road toward a pack that has passed it
- **Placement:** every kind starts in any lane, more often in the lanes left clear longest, so the sides are never safe (see [Spawning details](#spawning-details))
- **Randomness:** shared movement archetypes vary speed and timing: each mover's speed varies by up to about 25% either way (a dart's by up to 40%, and where it sets off is drawn apart from its speed, so a slow or a fast one can come from high up or close), each step or hop takes a little more or less time than the last, and the pause before each step, hop or dart varies widely too, so two movers of a kind can move differently. Exclusive paths vary their initial delay, mirror direction and repeat count where configured; their profile sets their movement speed and timing

Most movers use one of twelve movement archetypes, shared across neighbourhoods: Bounce, Cruise (in-lane), Dash and Swoop (darts: a swoop crosses at a tilt or may turn back; a dash crosses straight, as the ball does), Dive, Follow the leader, Hop, Merge, Stalk, Stop-and-go, Sway and Wander (steps).

- **Bounce:** a strict left-right hop, a lane to one side and then back, two to four hops, each after its own cue (the hopscotch kid, the sign spinner)
- **Cruise** (in-lane): keep their lane and move down the road at their own speed, faster than it scrolls (bikes, cars, the jogger, the Segway tour rider, the charging goose) or slower (the stroller, the garbage and ice cream trucks, the streetcar)
- **Dash and Swoop** (darts): cross the whole road (animals, such as the cat, the hen, the other dog and the squirrel). High Park's ferrets cross as a pair, 2 lanes wide. Each dart draws where it starts its crossing, from partway up the road to close to the pack, never so close that the pack has no time to react, so most crossings have to be dodged
  - **Angles:** the cat and the seagull always cross diagonally, coming down the road toward the pack, each at an angle it draws when it comes on, from a slight tilt to about 60 degrees, a steeper one starting farther up the road so it still leaves the pack time to react; in the third and fourth neighbourhoods of a walk, where the road goes by too fast for that, the steepest is about 50 and 40 degrees; the pigeons cross on a slight tilt up the road
  - **Turning back:** about a third of cats, ferrets, other dogs and seagulls turn back: somewhere from halfway to all the way across, drawn per dart, it turns with no cue of its own and crosses the whole road again the other way, keeping its tilt
  - **Stopping short:** some darting animals stop short instead, more or less often by how far the animal would really run: about half of cats (they freeze), a third of other dogs, and never a bird (pigeons, seagulls), a squirrel (so pulls stay simple) or the ball, which rolls straight across. One that stops does so with no cue: about three in four after 2, 3 or 4 lanes, drawn per dart, standing in that lane for the rest of its time on the road, and the rest on the far verge past the road's edge, out of play, where nothing can hit it. A dart that stops never turns back
- **Dive:** a dart that lands one to three lanes in from its edge, slides one lane further at a slower speed and stays down there; it never turns back (the frisbee dog, the volleyball player, which dives faster)
- **Follow the leader:** a group that steps a lane to either side as one block, each step after its leader's cue (the tour group behind the guide's flag, one or two steps)
- **Hop:** quick hops at random to a lane on either side, a row up the road, or diagonally up, never down toward the pack, 1 or 2 spots at a time. The bunny hops two to five times. Shy hoppers (the bunny) sometimes shy away instead: about half of them, when the pack draws near, hop away from its side of the road (a lane farther from the pack, or up), so they clear the pack's lane rather than block it
- **Merge:** an in-lane vehicle faster than the road that, partway down, signals and moves one lane over toward a side with room, then keeps going, always finishing before it reaches the pack (the delivery cyclist in Kensington Market, the Distillery District and on Yonge Street)
- **Stalk:** steps toward the pack while still ahead of it: a lane to either side, a row down, or diagonally down, whichever brings it closest. The thief and the raccoon step two or three times, the skunk three to six and the coyote three or four, the first a little after coming on screen
- **Stop-and-go:** starts standing, then pushes down its lane slower than the road, stops for a beat and goes on, three or four pushes, each after its own cue (the park ranger's cart, the brewery worker's keg dolly, the food cart and the ice cream bike)
  - **Crossers:** High Park's tourist train, the Distillery's brewery dray, The Beaches' patrol buggy (2 lanes wide) and Yonge Street's crosstown bus cross the road side-on, 3 lanes wide unless noted, slowly, each stopping once, the train for about a second and a half and the bus for about a second, the train ringing a bell and the bus opening its doors, before it goes on, always leaving the path's lane open. The train, the dray and the bus each stop covering the road's first three, middle three or last three lanes, so each may cover only part of the road, and the buggy on the second and third or the third and fourth lanes.
  - **Herring train:** about one in three tourist trains is a red herring instead: with no bell, it crosses the whole road far up the screen without stopping and is gone before its row nears the pack
- **Sway:** steps one lane over, pauses, and steps back, two or three times, rocking between two lanes; only its first step is cued (the couple, the stilt walker, the strolling busker)
- **Wander:** a few quick steps, each in any direction (a lane to either side, or a row up or down), with a short random pause and no cue between them (the mail carrier three or four, the tourist two to four)

The whole path is drawn before spawning, including its initial entry hold, mirror direction, starting position, timing variation and any repeat count. The mover holds at the road entrance through its initial delay and full first cue; the ducklings wait at their staged positions before their shared path clock starts. Its defining turns always remain. During their defining paths, the ducklings, rollerblader, rolling barrel and taxi advance continuously down the road more slowly than the road scrolls, with their lateral paths intact; their turns add no standstill pauses. The first move is cued for at least 0.6 seconds; later cues are as listed above. Every defining turn, including the last duckling's, completes at least 0.6 actual seconds before contact with the pack, accounting for each component's continuing velocity and footprint; normal scrolling, straight travel or an edge exit can continue afterwards. The planner reserves the actual swept hit boxes and keeps the reachable path, two open lanes and Muddy-pack reaction gaps. A signature path is selected only for a slot that safely accommodates its full route; it never substitutes ordinary movement. Ducklings are one logical obstacle: any duckling can cause its ordinary bump, and one hit removes the whole chain from play. After the last follower completes the route, all three ducklings idle, keep their lateral offsets and scroll normally with the road. These paths change no bump, card or contact effect.

Each mover draws its move count within its kind's range. Every person and animal on the road moves except the busker, the porch cat and the wedding photo shoot, which stand still. In-lane vehicles stay in their lanes except the taxi and delivery cyclist, which merge. Crossers follow the separate paths above. Benches, hedges, parked cars, puddles and trash cans stay fixed.

## Distractions

Each neighbourhood has 17 opportunities for a distraction, one in each equal stretch of road. Placements without clearance may be skipped. A distraction is a red herring that looks like one of the neighbourhood's own hazards. Most often it steps onto an edge lane near the top of the road, stands there for a quarter of its on-screen time and steps back off; otherwise it is already standing in an edge lane and lingers before stepping off, or now and then waits at the road's edge and leaves. It is gone well before its row nears the pack. It never shows a mover's cue, so a hazard that cues is always real. Red herrings never touch anything, never stand on a pickup, an obstacle or a roadside prop, and leave the road's plan as it is.

## Spawning details

How the planner places the road so it keeps the [Spawning](#spawning) and [Moving hazards](#moving-hazards) rules. The planner's release floor, search, certification and capacity limits, and the terms they use, are in [Planner work](../docs/planner-work.md#contract).

- **Admission:** every enabled positive-weight kind in the encounter and minor-obstacle pools appears at least once per neighbourhood visit; the other obstacles are drawn by their neighbourhood weights from the kinds that fit. A guarantee, an authored kind or role minimum, or a kind's full movement that cannot be placed fails planning: the walk never gets a plan without it, with another kind in its place or with simpler movement. Counts are logical kinds and groups, not skins or components: a three-bird duckling trail counts once
- **Counts:** each neighbourhood configures requested totals for fixed encounter-pool obstacles, moving encounter-pool obstacles, fixed minor obstacles and moving minor obstacles at each walk position. The obstacle-spawn modifier scales the combined total once, rounded to the nearest integer; largest remainders allocate it across the four roles, with ties in that order. The encounter roles cover the whole neighbourhood encounter pool, including puddles, potholes and squirrels, not only choice cards. Fixed roles have no assigned movement; moving roles keep the kind's full movement. These are requested role totals with per-kind minimums, delivered down to the release floor; guarantees, including the signature mover, consume the existing role budgets without increasing density and never raise a smaller budget. Pickups, lucky finds and gear retain their separate budgets. High Park requests 80 obstacles and Yonge Street 137 before modifiers. The four intermediate neighbourhoods request slightly more in position three than in position two.
- **Gaps:** wherever the path changes lanes between two obstacles, the road between their hit boxes, as drawn, is at least the tallest pack's height plus the one or two lane changes it makes there at a Muddy pack's speed, about a third of the slowest pack's, plus about half a second to react. Off the path, obstacles may stand closer. Two obstacles 3 lanes wide never sit in neighbouring rows: at least one empty row separates them. Narrower ones may, wherever the gap rule and the 2 open lanes per row still hold
- **Mover limits:** at most ten movers are on screen at once (at most six darting and four in-lane movers slower than the road); squirrels and the friendly neighbour block no lane and count only among themselves, against the same limits
- **Mover placement:**
  - **Crossers:** each crosser's stop and movement are chosen from those that keep the path open
  - **Darts:** a dart may set off from a spot something else already holds, crossing over or past it, so a fixed obstacle may stand, and an in-lane mover may pass, where only a dart will cross. A dart that blocks a lane (any dart but a squirrel) never blocks the path while it meets the pack, and obstacles may stand on the row before its crossing like any other
  - **Full movement:** placement preserves the path, open lanes, screen caps and reaction gaps, counting every lane a stalker or shy hopper may step or hop into. A mover never loses its sideways movement so that more obstacles fit, and a signature path is never replaced with ordinary movement
  - **Lanes:** two in-lane movers may share a lane when they never meet, always at least a cell's length apart
- **Path:** each neighbourhood draws its path first: it holds each lane for at least 2 rows, its lane stays open in every row, and every lane it crosses to change lanes stays clear in the row before the change. It carries on from the last neighbourhood's lane, and a walk starts it in the pack's lane. Wherever lane-blocking spawns wall off a stretch of road in a row, that stretch keeps a lane no dart sweeps, so a pack that dodged into it is never trapped
- **Quiet stretches:** at most 3 rows in a row come on screen with no lane-blocking obstacle in view. A quiet stretch from a neighbourhood's first row or to its last runs at most half that limit, rounded down (1 row), so two neighbourhoods' quiet ends never add up past it; a neighbourhood whose rows start in view (the walk's first) has none at its start
- **Visible kind limits:** each hazardous obstacle kind has at most two visible logical instances, fixed and moving together; skins and directions share the same kind limit. Visibility includes entry cues and conservatively allows for post-hit stopped scenery until the last visible part leaves. Each three-bird duckling trail, its leader and two followers, counts as one logical obstacle, so two trails may be visible together. While another instance of the same kind is visible, an incoming instance uses a different leftmost logical spawn lane and entry row, with a seeded entry stagger of at least one game step (the road's scroll in a 60th of a second). The entry row is the entry scroll distance divided by row spacing, rounded down. These separation rules apply at spawn only; later movement paths may cross naturally
