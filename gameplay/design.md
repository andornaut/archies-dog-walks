# Game Design

The rulebook for the first complete game, set in one world, Toronto. Targets, tuning and method are in [`README.md`](README.md), and undecided ideas are in [`ideas.md`](ideas.md).

[`src/data/`](../src/data/) holds every number and all content (names, text, prices, shapes, weights), and the [data page](https://archie.tristamittermayer.com/data/) shows them with the generated card text. This doc states the rules, with numbers where they define one. `$` and `♥` give the direction and rough size of a change (`$$$` and `♥♥` are bigger).

## Pitch

A cozy pixel-art lane runner: you are a dog walker walking a pack of dogs through the neighbourhood to make money.

## Pillars

1. **Cozy, not frantic:** calm speed, forgiving failure, "one more walk" appeal; playable for long stretches without stress
2. **Bumps add up:** a bump costs a heart and a card obstacle brings a choice; a walk ends when you finish, head home, or the last owner fires you
3. **Charming chaos:** cute dogs, dramatic owners, silly neighbourhood

## Core loop

- **Before a walk:** pick a walker, your dogs (up to one per owned dog slot) and up to one owned gear item for your Pocket.
- **During a walk:** walk 4 of the 6 neighbourhoods, starting in High Park and ending on Yonge Street. The street scrolls toward you; switch lanes to dodge obstacles and grab pickups. A plain obstacle costs a heart, or brings a debuff for a puddle or contact obstacle; a card obstacle opens a choice card.
- **Between neighbourhoods:** a neighbourhood celebration that pays the dogs, then a fork: each path leads to a neighbourhood and most carry an upgrade card, so one pick sets both.
- **After a walk:** bank your cash and spend your earnings at the Pet shop: dogs, dog slots and gear.

Reference points: Bowling Ballers (lane runner, but calmer), Hades (areas, run structure, loadout, meta progression), Vampire Survivors (choices along the way), Slay the Spire (choices as cards).

## Voice

- **Tone:** cozy, gentle and kid-friendly; the chaos is charming, the owners are dramatic, and no line mocks the player. Canadian spelling (colour, favourite, grey, neighbour), one name per thing (the walk's money is "cash"), spelled one way everywhere, so a name too long for its space is renamed everywhere rather than shortened in one place, ♥ right after its number ("+1♥", "{hearts}♥"), and no em dashes. Plain ASCII only, apart from ♥, · and the characters `src/ui/pixelFont.ts` maps (−, × and curly quotes): the pixel font has no other glyphs
- **Fit:** text never shrinks to fit. Every string is written to fit its box at that box's one text size; where a string can't be cut to fit, its box grows. Nothing steps a font down for a long line
- **Lengths:** message strip lines and first-time hints fit the strip's 2 rows at full size, with or without a debuff bar (312 px a row at 2×, about 36 characters); the walker's bubble about 30 characters a line, growing to fit its lines, with walker's lines, owner parting lines and notices at 2; card option names 1 to 4 words; hit labels one or two uppercase words with "!"
- **Walkers:** Ada calm and precise, Casey warm and encouraging, Mr. B easygoing, Trista sporty and quick
- **Owners** (each with 2 lines for each of happier, upset, fired and parting):
  - **Clare** (Lady): psychologist, quiet and friendly, gentle observations, a little shy
  - **Eve** (Ginger): YouTuber, everything is a video (vlog, views, subscribers)
  - **Gadget** (Penny): nervous but kind, double-checks everything, apologizes even when firing you
  - **Jason** (Buddy): lawn care pro, laid-back, yard talk (mowing, edging, weeds)
  - **Aunt Judy** (Sequoia): tennis star, older and athletic, tennis talk (aces, faults, match point)
  - **Kiwi** (Archie): unflappable grandmother, "dear" and "pet", offers cookies and tea
  - **Ralph** (Kilo): retired pilot, big and gruff, few words, flying talk (turbulence, landings, mayday)
  - **Ryan** (Reggie): engineer, practical, specs, structures and drawing boards
  - **Ms. Tara** (Pepe): dog-show judge, curt and formal, scores and deductions, a little French

## Rules

### Screen and controls

- Web, phone browser first. Touch and keyboard; mouse is not planned
- Portrait 360×560, ¾ top-down view, 5 lanes of 64px (the road spans x 20 to 340 at the pack). The road is drawn in pseudo-3D: lanes converge toward the backdrop band and things shrink with distance, with about 3.6 seconds of road visible ahead at the first neighbourhood's speed. The road keeps a hard edge where it meets the backdrop band. Lanes, distances and collisions are unchanged by it. The game fills the screen's width or height on every device, phones and tablets alike, at any scale, and sits at the top under the status bar in every orientation, with all the spare height below the dog cards. On a phone or tablet held sideways the game turns with the device and stays upright for it, rather than asking the player to turn the device back; a computer with a mouse never turns it
- **Layout,** from the top: the top bar, 40 rows tall like every screen's (big cash; the effect icons in six wells: the walker's perk, the Pocket item, found gear and up to 3 upgrades, filling left to right; then Help, Settings and Pause, left to right; mute is in Settings and on the M key); the debuff bars, when any is active; a pill naming the neighbourhood with its place in the walk ("2 of 4"), 4 rows under the last debuff bar, or under the top bar when none is active, moving as debuffs start and end; a backdrop band showing the current neighbourhood, with the message strip over its clouds, drawn over the debuff bars and the pill; the road across the full width, scrolling all the way to the top of its area; and a row of doghouse cards along the bottom, one per dog slot, with the neighbourhood progress line under them along the screen's bottom edge: a 2 px bar, bordered like the debuff bars, that fills left to right with the share of the current neighbourhood walked and starts empty again in the next. Cards, the pause menu and Settings dim the walk view (the backdrop band and the road); the top bar and doghouse cards stay lit
- **Doghouse cards:** slim cards (120×70) in pack order (the lead dog, then the left and right dogs): a name header, the dog's face, the owner's face (see Owner faces) and name with their hearts, and a LEAD tag on the lead dog. A fired dog's card keeps the owner's face, looking fired. The owner pops in as a photo tab when something happens to them (a heart change, the firing moment); the firing moment carries its own quip, and every other quip shows in the message strip. A still hold on a card shows its dog's size, hearts and owner
- **Loading:** startup, boot and walk progress use the 360×4 px bottom strip. On the home screen, pending activity and the Start walk wait use that bar at rows 147–150 in the yard, with a 1-row ink keyline top and bottom inside those rows, directly above the divider (rows 152–154) with row 151 clear; while showing it crosses the yard and the walker's legs, and it is hidden while idle. Progress fills left to right for settled files or completed tasks, holds full for 250 ms, then fades out over 150 ms, reduced motion included; waits without a known total show a segment travelling left to right on a repeating cycle until done, then disappear at once, reduced motion included. A bar shows only for a load the player waits for: boot, entering an area, the first visit's offline download, and the newer version a newer save needs; a background load never shows one. Offline registration and installation begin after home appears without blocking play; on a first visit (no installed version when the page loaded) they show the same travelling segment while active, and on a return visit registration and update downloads show nothing
- **Pausing:** the pause menu offers Resume, Help, Settings and End walk in one vertical stack of equal-width buttons. Keyboard focus stays inside the pause menu: Tab and Shift+Tab move between its buttons and its close control, Enter presses the focused one, and keys 1 to 4 press Resume, Help, Settings and End walk. The walk pauses whenever something takes the player's attention: the pause menu, Settings and Help, the End the walk? question, a card, the neighbourhood celebration, anything held for its explanation, and the tab being hidden or the window losing focus. Turning the device during a walk pauses the walk, as switching away does. Coming back to a hidden tab shows the pause menu, so the walk resumes only on Resume. While the walk is paused, everything in it holds: the road, the pack, the heart pops and other change labels, and the message strip's lines, which age only while walking. Hints and achievement toasts don't pause: they show in the message strip. While any card or overlay is open, only the walk bar's buttons respond, as they do with nothing open: the effect wells, the debuff bars and the dog cards explain themselves only with nothing open. Under an overlay that closes, a tap on them closes it as any tap outside its panel does; under a choice card, Neighbourhood done or New walker, it does nothing (see Panels)
- **Hold to explain:** during a walk, holding anything that explains itself (an effect icon, a debuff bar, a dog card), or tapping an effect icon, opens its explanation in a detail card, the same one the home screen's Details show, never in the message strip, and pauses the walk. The card stays up after the finger lifts; a tap anywhere closes it and resumes the walk. A tap on a walk bar button also presses that button; any other tap does nothing else
- **End walk:** the pause menu's End walk and the fork card's End walk ask "End the walk?", always showing how much of the cash ending the walk keeps, in the same words as the pause menu's note under End walk ("Ending now keeps $45 of $70", or "Ending now keeps all $70" when it keeps it all); under it, when ending gives anything up, what it gives up in red: what the current neighbourhood has earned (tips, lucky finds and treat cash, less what it has spent; none at a fork); then the number of neighbourhoods left after this one when any are. Keep walking is the default (key 1); End walk is key 2. End walk ends the walk (see A walk) and keeps what the finished neighbourhoods earned
- **Drag** (the main touch control): put a thumb down anywhere and drag. The first lane step comes after a quarter of a lane-width of travel, and the Nth at N lane-widths from where the drag started, so the thumb never covers the walker. Dragging back a quarter of a lane-width from the furthest point turns the drag around, counting again from there. The pack moves lane by lane toward the target at the walker's lane-change speed
- **Tap:** moves one lane toward the tapped half of the road, as soon as the finger lands; on the doghouse row, when it lifts, so holding a card only explains it. A drag shorter than the first lane step counts as a tap, and a drag whose first step goes against the tap takes the tap back, so the pack ends where the drag alone puts it
- **Keyboard:** ←/→, A/D or H/L; one lane per press; holding a key doesn't repeat
- While moving between lanes, the pack can hit things in either lane. A reverse press turns the pack around at once; each other press queues one more lane
- Taps on the top bar never move the pack; taps and drags on the doghouse row steer like anywhere on the road. During a walk, every part of the screen below the top bar steers, the margins around the game included: a drag or tap there acts as one at the nearest edge of the game area, and the top bar and the margin beside or above it keep their own input

### The pack and hits

- **Formation:** the walker is at the bottom, the dogs close ahead in the same lane on visible leashes, every dog readable. The game sets the formation by size, never the player: the biggest dog leads in front (drawn behind the side dogs) and is the lead dog, the next biggest walks on the left and the smallest on the right; ties go by roster order (alphabetical by name). Two dogs walk side by side out front, a little less far forward than a 3-dog lead, the bigger on the left as the lead dog. One dog walks centred in the lead spot. No dog's hit box reaches into the next lane, so a pack fully in a lane never touches an obstacle in the lane beside it
- **Hit boxes:** one rectangle per dog and one for the walker, each a few pixels inside its sprite; leashes can't be hit. Obstacle sprites are drawn at 48 px, and each obstacle hits as drawn: 2 px inside its opaque outline, at its depth, within its own lanes. Touching any part of an obstacle counts as one hit. Once an obstacle is below the walker's feet it can no longer hit, however many rows it spans. Passing a mover is irreversible: once below the walker's feet, it cannot move back up into the pack or hit later, even if it has never been hit
- **Who a hit lands on:** a bump lands on the dog whose rectangle touched: if only the walker's did, on the lead dog; if several dogs' did, on the one nearest the contact. Card obstacles hit the whole pack. Pickups (tips, treats and lucky finds) collect anywhere in the pack's whole outline, leashes included. Lane changes sweep the pack, so a fast move across several lanes can't skip a hazard or a pickup
- **Bumps:** running into a plain obstacle with no contact effect is a bump, and costs 1♥ from the owner of the dog it lands on. Puddles, potholes and contact obstacles cost no heart and bring their debuff instead (see Encounters and debuffs); card obstacles open a card. While Spotlight lasts, a bump also costs cash; otherwise cash goes down only through a card option you pick, and an early end gives up what the current neighbourhood has earned (see Cash)
- **After a hit:** each spawned obstacle can cause at most one bump across all of its components, even if its movement reverses. The whole obstacle is out of play at once but stays on the road as scenery (a trash can rocking, a mover stopped) and scrolls off with it, touching nothing. Obstacles are ignored for a 1-second grace window, during which the walker and all dogs blink together, or remain steadily dimmed with reduced motion; pickups still count
- **Hit stop:** a hit freezes the road and the pack briefly while it plays (about 1 second for a card obstacle, before its card; about 0.3 seconds for a plain obstacle or a puddle). Input is ignored during the stop, and it doesn't count as walk time. Lucky finds don't stop the walk

### Spawning

- Spawning always leaves a reachable path, accounting for every mover's moves, so every hit is avoidable (see Spawning details)
- Scroll speed and obstacle density rise gently each neighbourhood
- Obstacles span lanes and rows (shapes in the data). None is wider than 3 lanes, every row leaves at least 2 lanes open, and obstacle rows leave room for the pack's full length.
- The Distillery District has fixed obstacles 1 and 2 lanes wide. Every other neighbourhood has fixed obstacles of all three widths: at least one 1 lane wide, one 2 lanes wide and one 3 lanes wide
- Obstacles, fixed and moving, go toward the lanes left clear longest, so no lane, the sides included, stays clear for long
- The first neighbourhood's first obstacle reaches the pack about two seconds in at the soonest, so a walk never opens with a hit

### Pulls

Every dog pulls toward squirrels. A squirrel is no hit and opens no card: touching one does nothing, it darts across as an animal mover, and the pull is its only cost. Since nothing has to steer around it, a squirrel blocks no lane: the road keeps no room clear for it, it may dart across where obstacles stand, and no tip, treat or lucky find lies on its path. A dog pulls the pack one lane only toward a squirrel whose cue has started (one waiting at the road's edge draws no pull yet) and that is still ahead of the pack, and one dog strains at a time, the one nearest the squirrel side to side. If the straining dog's owner fires you, the strain ends and that squirrel draws no more. The dog strains for about 1 second, with a bubble over it, an arrow and a line to the squirrel, and a taut leash; then the pack lurches one lane toward the squirrel, unless you steer away during the strain, which cancels the pull; in an edge lane, pressing toward the edge counts as steering away. The message strip announces the strain as its cue; a cancel is quiet. The pull goes toward the squirrel's side as it is when the pack lurches, so a squirrel that crosses changes it, and a squirrel in the pack's own lane keeps the dog straining with no pull until it moves out of that lane. A pull never moves the pack into an obstacle it can't react to: if a lane-blocking obstacle or mover in the target lane would reach the pack within about two lane moves and a moment to react, the dog keeps straining and pulls once the lane clears. The strain ends with no pull if the squirrel goes first: off the road past the edge beside the pack, into the pack's rows, or away with the straining dog when its owner fires you.

### Resources

- **Money ($):** the only currency, in whole dollars. A multiplied amount rounds to the nearest dollar
  - **Cash:** what you carry during the walk. It starts at $0 and grows with tips and lucky finds; card options are paid from it. It never goes below $0. What the walk banks depends on how it ends: a finished walk banks all its cash; heading home, or every owner firing you, banks the cash held at the end of the last finished neighbourhood, losing what the current one has earned since. Card options spend the current neighbourhood's earnings first, then the earlier cash, so a walk whose card costs spent into what it would keep keeps what's left. The end screen shows what a walk lost as a Cash lost line. Settling a walk never reduces the saved bank balance, whether the walk finishes, ends early, loses every dog, or is interrupted and cannot resume
  - **Tips:** a tip is $1. Tips add to the pay, whatever the pack size, and they thin out across a walk: the first neighbourhood spawns the most, and each later one 3 fewer (31 down to 22), before each neighbourhood's tip density scales them (a fifth more in Kensington Market). Each neighbourhood sets its own obstacle counts for each walk position, fixed and moving, card obstacles and plain ones: 80 in High Park, rising through the neighbourhoods to 140 on Yonge Street, and Cabbagetown, Kensington Market, The Beaches and the Distillery District place a few more as a walk's third neighbourhood than as its second. Plain obstacles are fixed ones (benches, carts, hedges, parked cars, trash cans and each neighbourhood's own) and movers. No stretch of more than 3 rows of road comes into view without a visible obstacle in it (a mover counts only once its body is on the road, not from its cue, and a squirrel never, as it blocks no lane), so the road never looks quiet for long, while busy bursts and calmer stretches still come at random.
  - **Pay:** the steady part. After each neighbourhood, each walking dog earns $20, the same in every neighbourhood. Pay joins the cash at the end of each neighbourhood (at its celebration, or on the end screen after neighbourhood 4); the cash held then is what an early end keeps. A firing loses that dog's pay for the current neighbourhood and the Walk bonus; its earlier pay stays in the cash
- **Owner happiness (♥):** one meter per dog, shown on its doghouse card; it never relies on colour alone. The dog's size sets its max (see Dogs); a smaller pack adds to its lead dog's owner's max, by the pack the walk sets out with, and a firing never moves it: setting out with 1 dog, its owner gets +2♥; with 2, the lead dog's owner gets +1♥; with 3, nobody does. No owner's max goes above 6♥, whatever adds to it. While the pack chosen earns it, the start screen's Dogs band says so. Every heart looks the same, whatever adds it: the extra hearts a bonus adds (the small-pack bonus, Lucky collar) are drawn like the rest, and holding the card names each source. Every owner starts full, and treats restore hearts up to the max. At 0 the owner fires you with a quip and the dog leaves
- **Every change shows its cause:** each ♥ change shows its amount over the dog and on its doghouse card ("−1♥"), with its cause in the message strip, and each $ change a label with its amount alone ("+$1", "−$20"; see Heart changes and Money changes); a card's enlarged view lists the modifiers behind its numbers

### A walk

- A walk is 4 neighbourhoods of Toronto. Each neighbourhood is the same distance; the first takes about 2½ minutes and later ones less as speed rises, about 8 minutes of walking and about 9 in all with cards, stops and celebrations
- After neighbourhoods 1 to 3, the neighbourhood celebration, then a fork. Neighbourhood 4 has no fork
- **Forks:** the road meets a cross street; after neighbourhoods 1 and 2 a fork card offers three paths (left, straight, right), each leading to a neighbourhood you haven't walked this walk other than Yonge Street, sorted easiest on the left to hardest on the right, ties in table order. The fork after neighbourhood 3 leads only to Yonge Street, so it shows one path. Each path card shows the neighbourhood's name and difficulty, and the upgrade card it carries: the upgrade's name over a few words on what it does, said in full on the enlarged card. At a fork with more than one path, one path, drawn at random, carries no upgrade, and its card says "No upgrade" with no plaque; Yonge Street's path after neighbourhood 3 always carries one. The fork card's subtitle reads "Most paths bring an upgrade". Picking a path picks the next neighbourhood and its upgrade, if any, at once. An End walk button under the paths ends the walk there (see End walk)
- **Neighbourhoods** (6, each walk visits 4 with no repeats):

Cabbagetown, The Beaches, the Distillery District and Yonge Street each also have their own card obstacle (see Encounters and debuffs), and every neighbourhood has a few obstacles and movers found nowhere else; no mover or plain obstacle but the squirrel appears in more than 3 neighbourhoods, each street cart look counting as its own.

Each neighbourhood's roster meets the [neighbourhood variety target](README.md#targets), counted as defined there; a moving and a parked car share one visual family, and distinct street cart looks count separately. The Distillery District has 24 visual families, 7 fixed kinds and 17 moving kinds. The planned mover share of all road obstacles rises from about 50% in High Park through 52% in Cabbagetown, 54% in Kensington Market, 56% in The Beaches and 58% in the Distillery District to 60% on Yonge Street; the fork position can shift these shares slightly. Shared movement archetypes and each neighbourhood's own characters provide the rest of the mix. At the same walk position, total obstacle counts increase from High Park through Cabbagetown, Kensington Market, The Beaches and the Distillery District to Yonge Street.

The street cart, a 2-lane obstacle that never moves, stands in every neighbourhood with that neighbourhood's look: flowers in Cabbagetown, fruit and veg in Kensington Market, hot dogs in High Park and Kensington Market, ice cream in the Distillery District and The Beaches, and newspapers and magazines on Yonge Street. Where a neighbourhood has two looks, each cart picks one.

- The walk ends after neighbourhood 4, on End walk, or when no dogs are left. What it banks depends on how it ends (see Cash)
- **Interrupted walk:** the game saves the walk at each fork, as each neighbourhood starts, and whenever it's hidden. A walk closed before it ends resumes on the next launch at the start of the neighbourhood it was in, or at the fork it was waiting at, opening straight into the walk with the pause menu up; whatever happened in that neighbourhood before the close is undone and plays again. A saved walk that can't be resumed (it names a dog or neighbourhood the game no longer has, or the save is damaged) is banked as one that headed home
- **Walk bonus:** when every dog you set out with gets home at the end of neighbourhood 4, the walk pays a Walk bonus of $50 into the cash, whatever the pack size, shown on the end screen. Heading home early or a firing loses it
- **End screen:** the walk's total with a breakdown (pay, tips, lucky finds, Walk bonus, card costs, and in red the cash lost when the walk ended mid-neighbourhood), every row shown and a zero reading "$0" with no sign and the neighbourhoods walked, with no bank; a new-best ribbon when it beats the best walk with the same number of dogs; the badges the walk earned, each opening its badge card on a tap or a hold, in one row up to 7 and two rows past that; one closing line from each owner (a happier line from owners still walking, the fired line from owners who fired you). The results panel sits below the returning group where its allocated content fits, and the whole badge panel is centred in the remaining space above the return button, with at least 3 px of button clearance

### Choice cards

- Used for encounters and forks. Up to three across, art-led: an illustration plus a one-line effect; hold to enlarge for details
- Every card hits the whole pack. No card opens without a collision, except the fork card. A hit that opens a card costs nothing until you choose
- Each option's outcome is fixed and shown on the card, with hearts per dog ("−1♥ Ginger"); a card for two or three dogs reads "−1♥ each" unless Second chance would forgive one of them, when it names each dog ("♥ Ginger's safe!, −1♥ Kilo"), and its enlarged view names each dog. A perk, gear item or upgrade that changes an option shows its charm centred just under that option's card: the item's icon on the left and, beside it, the item's name over what it did to that option (Hall pass's skip "Free skip", Quick recovery "Half time", Second chance "Safe", Charmer "Free"), with two charms stacked, the enlarged card included, never on the card, so the card keeps its full text box. The card row (title, cards and charms) rises to the 2/3 line of the walk view. Choices only, no minigames
- A money option shows its price on its plate, greyed out when you can't afford it, so cash never goes below $0; every card keeps at least one option that costs no money, and its plate is blank. Money options are charged once per card, whatever the pack size; heart options cost each owner, so a bigger pack has more owners who can fire you. Slower options cost money (missed tips) instead of time
- An option says what it would do before you pick: a debuff option states its stake ("Quick rinse → Stinky: tips pay nothing for 60s"), an option that would fire an owner says so ("Fires Jason"), and a heart Second chance would forgive reads as safe
- A fork's path card shows its upgrade's duration (Now, Next or All walk) where other cards show a price
- A card shows at most 3 options. Hall pass offers "Skip (Hall pass)" (free, skipping the card) on every encounter card until it's used in that neighbourhood, in place of the card's hearts option. Charmer and Hall pass can both apply to the same card: its money options are free and the skip is offered, and the player chooses; Charmer is used up by that card whichever option is picked
- After a card choice, each owner whose hearts changed reacts with a quip in the message strip

### Teaching, sound and settings

- **Back:** works the same in a browser tab and installed as an app. Each back closes the innermost thing open: a detail card (one held open during a walk closes as a tap closes it), Settings' install steps or the end screen's badge card, then the Pet shop, the Logbook, Help, Settings or the badge celebration. During a walk, back opens the pause menu, over a choice card or the neighbourhood celebration too; in the pause menu, it resumes the walk; on the End the walk? question, it goes back to where the question was asked: the pause menu, or the fork card. On the end screen with nothing open, it goes home. On the home screen with nothing open, it asks "Quit?" with Keep playing (the default, key 1) and Quit (key 2); Quit leaves as the top bar's Exit does: an installed app closes, and a browser tab goes to the site's home page. Back or a tap outside keeps playing. Between a walk's end and the end screen, back does nothing. Among installed apps, only one the game can close asks Quit? and has Exit: Chrome's Android app, and Chrome's and Edge's app windows on a computer, opened straight into the game. In every other installed app (iPhone and iPad Home Screen apps, and Firefox's, Samsung Internet's and Edge's Android apps), back is the browser's own: it never asks Quit?, closes nothing in the game, and leaves it, and the home top bar has no Exit and Q does nothing. In a Firefox for Android tab, and in browsers without close watchers (older Chrome, and Safari), back before the first tap or key press after the page loads is the browser's own and leaves the game
- **Buttons:** screen-wide buttons are 296×48 px; compact screen buttons are 128×48 px and count as standard. Every control's press area is at least 48×48 px, growing around a smaller face; where neighbouring buttons' grown press areas overlap, a press goes to the button whose centre is nearest. The debuff bars, which only explain on a hold, and the walk bar's 22 px effect wells, which explain on a tap, are exempt from the 48 px size. Standard buttons side by side keep 16 px between faces and stacked ones 8 px, as do a list's pressable rows, Settings' rows included, with any separator in the gap; where either neighbour is smaller than a standard button, the gap is 24 px in any direction. The home and walk top bars' buttons sit in 48 px columns and press 48 rows down from the screen's top, though a showing debuff bar takes its own rows from the walk bar's buttons. The walk bar's effect wells keep their own spacing (2 px apart), as do the choice cards (6 px apart), the doghouse cards (edge to edge) and the end screen's badge strip (16 px apart in 48 px columns). Modal action buttons are 48 px high, with a minimum width of their full-size text's visible ink plus 32 px, at least 48 px. Action button groups use a single horizontal row or a single vertical column. Every button in a vertical group shares the width required by its widest button. ### Pickups

- **Tip:** +$1
- **Treat:** a grab item, not carried. It goes to the dog nearest where it touched (the lead dog if only the walker touched it), whose owner gets +♥ up to the max; if that owner is already at max, the treat goes to the owner below their max with the fewest hearts (ties to the dog nearer the front), and if every owner is at max, the Fanny pack keeps it when it has room; otherwise the treat pays $5 of cash, a gain like any other (see Money changes), counted with the tips on the end screen but never as a tip, so Stinky and Big tipper don't change it

### Dogs

A roster of 9, three in each size, each a fixed, named character with its own art and one fixed owner. You start with 1 dog slot and Archie, and buy up to 2 more slots and the other 8 dogs at the Pet shop. Dog prices mix sizes: each price band ($200 to $400, $600 to $800, $1,100 to $2,200) has dogs of at least two sizes, so a cheap dog can be large and a pricey one small. Walking more dogs is the main progression: more pay and a longer-lasting walk, but more owners who can fire you.

Dogs differ in play by size only:

### Lucky finds

Good things to bump into on purpose. Each neighbourhood places 1 lost wallet and, half the time, a friendly neighbour; High Park and The Beaches add a quarter (a second wallet 1 in 4 times, a neighbour 5 in 8). Its finds spread evenly: two in its first and last thirds, three in its first, middle and last fifths. None opens a card: each bobs on the road and pays out on contact, with a short popup where it was grabbed and a quip from the owners who gained. The friendly neighbour walks about, one or two quick steps in any direction and with no cue, since a cue marks a hazard about to move, so the pack has to go and meet her; the lost wallet lies still.

- **Friendly neighbour:** pats for the pack, the hearts jackpot: 3♥ shared by the walking dogs, lead dog first (a lone dog's owner +3♥; a pair's lead dog +2♥ and the other +1♥; a trio +1♥ each), each owner capped at their max; a heart an owner can't take goes to the next owner below their max, lead dog first, and is lost only when every owner is full, so a smaller pack gains more per dog
- **Lost wallet:** +$$ found money, the money jackpot
- **Gear drop:** a gear item, about one a neighbourhood (on average 0.5, 0.8, 1.2 and 1.5 in neighbourhoods 1 to 4, two at most, never the same item twice in one), drawn among the gear not in effect, the cheaper more likely, in proportion to 1/price. Grabbing it puts it in effect for the rest of the walk on top of the Pocket item, owned or not; a later grab swaps it, and the earlier find goes back. Long reach doesn't reach it. A found item works from the moment it's grabbed: Hall pass from the current neighbourhood, the Fanny pack from the next treat it can keep, and Lucky collar raises owners' max ♥ but not their hearts (and lowers it again if swapped away, current hearts above it dropping to the max)

### Distractions

About 21 times a neighbourhood, a red herring: one of the neighbourhood's own people or animals, a kind that is a hazard there, most often steps onto an edge lane near the top of the road, lingers there a moment and steps back off; otherwise it is already standing in an edge lane and lingers before stepping off, or now and then waits at the road's edge and leaves. It is gone well before its row nears the pack. It never shows a mover's cue, so a hazard that cues is always real. Red herrings never touch anything, never stand on a pickup, an obstacle or a roadside prop, and leave the road's plan as it is.

### Moving hazards

Each obstacle uses its neighbourhood look, with any of its local skins equally likely. Some kinds can move (each kind's movement is in the data), and every one that can always moves. Art stays upright and readable while its position follows its path; a turn does not rotate its billboard into the road.

- **Cues:** a mover first warns before moving: its first move is telegraphed about 0.6 seconds ahead, and its later moves come with no cue of their own unless its movement below says otherwise, a stepper's and a hopper's each after a short random pause once the move before ends. A darting animal shows as soon as its stretch of road comes into view (at the road's top edge, if it sets off farther up), waiting at its edge until its cue. An in-lane mover's cue shows as soon as its stretch of road comes into view, so it can last longer than 0.6 seconds
- **Hits and pickups:** a mover may pass over a tip or a treat, which stays where it is, and may end a move on a tip, but never on a treat or a lucky find, so those can always be grabbed without a hit. A mover's hit is its standing obstacle's hit
- **Only ahead of the pack:** a mover moves only while it is still ahead of the pack: once it reaches the pack's rows it makes no more moves, and nothing ever moves back up the road toward a pack that has passed it
- **Placement:** every kind starts in any lane, more often in the lanes left clear longest, so the sides are never safe (see Spawning details)
- **Randomness:** shared movement archetypes vary speed and timing: each mover's speed varies by up to about 25% either way (a dart's by up to 40%, and where it sets off is drawn apart from its speed, so a slow or a fast one can come from high up or close), each step or hop takes a little more or less time than the last, and the pause before each step, hop or dart varies widely too, so two movers of a kind can move differently. Exclusive paths vary their initial delay, mirror direction and repeat count where configured; their profile sets their movement speed and timing

Most movers use one of twelve movement archetypes, shared across neighbourhoods: Bounce, Cruise (in-lane), Dash and Swoop (darts: a swoop crosses at a tilt or may turn back; a dash crosses straight, as the ball does), Dive, Follow the leader, Hop, Merge, Stalk, Stop-and-go, Sway and Wander (steps).

- **Bounce:** a strict left-right hop, a lane to one side and then back, two to four hops, each after its own cue (the hopscotch kid, the sign spinner)
- **Cruise** (in-lane): keep their lane and move down the road at their own speed, faster than it scrolls (bikes, cars, the jogger, the Segway tour rider, the charging goose) or slower (the stroller, the garbage and ice cream trucks, the streetcar)
- **Dash and Swoop** (darts): cross the whole road (animals, such as the cat, the hen, the other dog and the squirrel). High Park's ferrets cross as a pair, 2 lanes wide. Each dart draws where it starts its crossing, from partway up the road to close to the pack, never so close that the pack has no time to react, so most crossings have to be dodged
  - **Angles:** the cat and the seagull always cross diagonally, coming down the road toward the pack, each at an angle it draws when it comes on, from a slight tilt to about 60 degrees, a steeper one starting farther up the road so it still leaves the pack time to react; in the third and fourth neighbourhoods of a walk, where the road goes by too fast for that, the steepest is about 50 and 40 degrees; the pigeons cross on a slight tilt up the road
  - **Turning back:** about a third of cats, ferrets, other dogs and seagulls turn back: somewhere from halfway to all the way across, drawn per dart, it turns with no cue of its own and crosses the whole road again the other way, keeping its tilt
  - **Stopping short:** some darting animals stop short instead, more or less often by how far the animal would really run: about half of cats (they freeze), a third of other dogs, and never a bird (pigeons, seagulls), a squirrel (so pulls stay simple) or the ball, which rolls straight across. One that stops does so with no cue: about three in four after 2, 3 or 4 lanes, drawn per dart, standing in that lane for the rest of its time on the road, and the rest on the far verge past the road's edge, out of play, where nothing can hit it. A dart that stops never turns back
- **Dive:** a dart that lands one to three lanes in from its edge, slides one lane further at a slower speed and stays down there; it never turns back (the frisbee dog, the volleyball player, which dives faster)
- **Follow the leader:** a group that steps a lane to either side as one block, each step after its leader's cue (the tour group behind the guide's flag, one or two steps)
- **Hop:** quick hops at random to a lane on either side, a row up the road, or diagonally up, never down toward the pack, 1 or 2 spots at a time. The bunny hops two to five times. Shy hoppers (the bunny) sometimes shy away instead: about half of them, when the pack draws near, hop away from its side of the road (a lane farther from the pack, or up), so they clear the pack's lane rather than block it
- **Merge:** an in-lane vehicle faster than the road that, partway down, signals and moves one lane over toward a side with room, then keeps going, always finishing before it reaches the pack (the delivery cyclist in Kensington Market, the Distillery District and on Yonge Street)
- **Stalk:** steps toward the pack while still ahead of it: a lane to either side, a row down, or diagonally down, whichever brings it closest. The thief and the raccoon step two or three times, the skunk three to six and the coyote three or four, the first a little after coming on screen
- **Stop-and-go:** starts standing, then pushes down its lane slower than the road, stops for a beat and goes on, three or four pushes, each after its own cue (the park ranger's cart, the brewery worker's keg dolly, the food cart and the ice cream bike)
  - **Crossers:** High Park's tourist train, the Distillery's brewery dray, The Beaches' patrol buggy (2 lanes wide) and Yonge Street's crosstown bus cross the road side-on, 3 lanes wide unless noted, slowly, each stopping once, the train for about a second and a half and the bus for about a second, the train ringing a bell and the bus opening its doors, before it goes on, always leaving the path's lane open. The train, the dray and the bus each stop covering the road's first three, middle three or last three lanes, so each may cover only part of the road, and the buggy on the second and third or the third and fourth lanes.
  - **Herring train:** about one in three tourist trains is a red herring instead: with no bell, it crosses the whole road far up the screen without stopping and is gone before its row nears the pack
- **Sway:** steps one lane over, pauses, and steps back, two or three times, rocking between two lanes; only its first step is cued (the couple, the stilt walker, the strolling busker)
- **Wander:** a few quick steps, each in any direction (a lane to either side, or a row up or down), with a short random pause and no cue between them (the mail carrier three or four, the tourist two to four)

During their defining paths, the ducklings, rollerblader, rolling barrel and taxi advance continuously down the road more slowly than the road scrolls, with their lateral paths intact; their turns add no standstill pauses. Vehicles (bikes, cars, scooters, the streetcar and trucks) only ever move up or down their own lane, never diagonally, and never sideways except the merging taxi and delivery cyclist. Benches, hedges, parked cars, puddles and trash cans never move.

### Feedback

- **Bump flash:** non-dodged bumps and contact obstacles or movers, including puddles, potholes and contacts whose effects are prevented, share one 280 ms feedback sequence. An opaque 5 px border with a 1 px inner ink keyline and a 12% contact-colour road tint show during 0–80 ms and 120–200 ms. The road offsets (+5, +5) px in the first window, returns to zero during 80–120 ms, then offsets (−5, −5) px in the second window. During 200–280 ms the border and tint are off while the road settles linearly to zero at whole-pixel offsets. Only the border promotes amber and tan to yellow, cream and light grey to white, and mauve to pink; other vivid colours stay unchanged. Reduced motion shows one static 280 ms border and tint, without repeated flashing or displacement. A new hit resets and restarts the sequence; leaving the scene clears it. Contact-obstacle and contact-mover splashes and debris appear below actors, including for prevented effects. Contacts of the same obstacle kind share one feedback event per rendered frame, including across multiple fixed steps. Material sound plays at most once per cue per 100 ms across the pack
- **Debuff contact cues:** when an obstacle or mover contact actually applies or refreshes Muddy, Spotlight, Stinky or Zoomies, one native 60×36 px cue displayed at 2× (120×72 px) in both motion modes follows the feet of the dog leading the formation when the cue begins, above ground shadows and below all actors and leashes. Each cue uses the exact colour of its effect's progress bar, plays twelve 100 ms frames once for 1,200 ms without repeating, and is clipped to the road rather than moved away from the feet. Reduced motion holds peak frame 4 for the full 1,200 ms. The matching native 16×16 px HUD icon appears at 2× (32×32 px), without a plate, with its bottom 4 px above the lead dog's opaque top. Its position is captured once and kept inside the viewport; it neither follows the pack nor rises, and fades away over 1,000 ms in both motion modes. Distinct debuff icons sit without overlap, with 4 px gaps; another popup of the same kind replaces its existing icon. One sound for each acquired or refreshed effect kind plays at onset through the existing mute and volume controls; actual applications or refreshes of the same debuff kind share one ground cue, icon popup and sound per rendered frame across multiple fixed steps, with no duplicate sounds per pack member. Distinct debuff kinds and applications in the next frame can produce their own cues; coalescing feedback does not change the applied effects. Prevented effects and card outcomes produce no debuff-acquisition ground cue, icon popup or matching effect sound; physical material audio remains separate
- **Effect icons:** upgrades in effect, the walker's perk, the Pocket item and found gear show as small icons in the walk bar's wells. Tap or hold one to see what it does. ### Upgrade cards

Walk-only boosts on the fork paths, from a pool of 6. Each fork draws its upgrades from the walk's seed, never from how the walk went: one path of a fork with more than one, at random, carries none, and every other path carries one drawn at random from the remaining pool, none twice and at most one trade-off. Picking a path takes its upgrade, which leaves the pool for the rest of the walk; "next neighbourhood" is the one that path leads to. Effects without "now" or "next neighbourhood" last the rest of the walk.

- Belly rubs: +1♥ to every owner now, up to their max
- Big tipper: every tip is worth $1 more in the next neighbourhood (a Stinky tip still pays $0)
- Charmer: the first card in each neighbourhood has free money options (hearts and debuffs still cost; see Choice cards)
- Good leash: no dog pulls toward squirrels
- Raincoats: puddles and potholes don't make the pack Muddy: no Muddy and no hit stop. A card's Muddy still applies
- Rush hour (trade-off): twice the tips and treats and 30% more obstacles in the next neighbourhood

### Walkers

Four walkers. You start with Casey; each other walker unlocks the first time you finish a walk with at least so many dogs still walking at the end (see New walker): Mr. B 1, Ada 2 and Trista 3, so a walk finished with 3 dogs unlocks all three. Every walker walks the same road at the same speed, and a walker's effects stack with the Pocket item and upgrade cards.

**Agility** sets how fast the pack changes lanes, as a multiple of the base lane-change speed. Walkers spread from 1.0 to 1.4, Running shoes add 1.0, each small dog in the pack adds 0.1 and each large dog takes 0.1 off, never below 0.9. Players never see the word agility, only a percentage against the base, amount first, in the same words everywhere: walker cards say "+40% lane change speed" (Ada) or "+10% lane change speed" (Casey), Running shoes "+100% lane change speed", a small dog "+10% lane change speed" and a large dog "−10% lane change speed"; a walker at the base speed (Mr. B) shows no lane-change line. The slowest pack's lane move (the 0.9 floor) sets the row spacing.

Each walker has one **perk**:

- **Keen eye** (Mr. B): 50% more tips and treats appear
- **Long reach** (Trista): the lead dog grabs tips and treats one lane to either side
- **Quick recovery** (Ada): debuffs last half as long
- **Second chance** (Casey): the first ♥ lost in each neighbourhood is forgiven (one heart; never Spotlight's cash); when several owners lose one at once, it forgives the owner left with the fewest hearts, ties to the lead dog

### Spawning details

How the planner places the road so it keeps the Spawning and Moving hazards rules.

- **Admission:** the planner reserves safe obstacle slots before selecting compatible kinds by their neighbourhood weights. Every enabled positive-weight kind in the encounter and minor-obstacle pools appears at least once per neighbourhood visit. These minimum guarantees reserve their slots first; remaining slots use weighted compatible full profiles. Each candidate uses its full movement profile, footprint and visibility lifetime; selection preserves the path, open lanes, screen caps, reaction gaps and signature movement. Preflight rejects malformed counts, pools or guarantees, role budgets below their guarantees, and modified totals above capacity as invalid configurations. Bounded construction exhaustion reports that no plan was found, not that the configuration is impossible. The final independent audit must accept every placement; an invariant defect blocks the result. A runtime plan is released only as an independently certified layout containing at least 90% of the neighbourhood's original combined active and auxiliary requests, measured against the original configured quotas. That is a release floor; runtime generation targets 100% delivery (see [README](README.md#release-and-tuning)). Optional requests, including rewards, may be omitted down to that floor. Guarantees, authored kind and role minima, full movement profiles, native safety and visible quiet coverage are never omitted, and a substitute kind or movement downgrade earns no credit. A minimum that cannot be placed causes explicit planning failure, never a plan without it, a substitute kind or a movement downgrade. Counts are logical kinds and groups, not skins or components: a three-bird duckling trail counts once
- **Counts:** each neighbourhood configures requested totals for fixed encounter-pool obstacles, moving encounter-pool obstacles, fixed minor obstacles and moving minor obstacles at each walk position. The obstacle-spawn modifier scales the combined total once, rounded to the nearest integer; largest remainders allocate it across the four roles, with ties in that order. The encounter roles cover the whole neighbourhood encounter pool, including puddles, potholes and squirrels, not only choice cards. Fixed roles have no assigned movement; moving roles retain the kind's full movement profile. These are requested role totals with per-kind minimums, delivered within the Admission floor; guarantees, including the signature mover, consume the existing role budgets without increasing density and never raise a smaller budget. Capacity is a validation-only ceiling after modifiers. Extra capacity slots cannot change the active layout and never appear on the road. Pickups, lucky finds and gear retain their separate budgets
- **Search budget:** the planner first tries the original order. An independently certified layout from that attempt that meets the Admission floor, complete or partial, is accepted. After a floor-meeting partial layout is found, search continues only within that attempt, pruned by a dominance bound; nothing is final until the layout is certified. Within each attempt, an optional request is omitted only after many failed placement attempts. If the attempt has no certified floor-meeting layout once either counter has used most of the allowance available to that attempt, it restarts from an empty layout and omits an optional request after a few failed attempts for the rest of that allowance. The planner makes at most one balanced fallback attempt, only when the original attempt fails on construction. It skips the fallback when the original attempt hits a template, anchor, invalid-profile or invariant failure, or is blocked with an unattributed failure. Candidate checks and propagation checks are metered separately, each with a per-attempt cap and a cumulative cap across both attempts; anchors and compiled templates have cumulative caps too. [`docs/planner-work.md`](../docs/planner-work.md) holds the thresholds and caps. Both attempts keep the same anchors, templates, slots, quotas, frozen movement and random draws, guarantees and native safety. Invariant failures report as invariant failures; otherwise, when no attempt yields such a layout, the planner reports construction exhaustion
- **Gaps:** wherever the path changes lanes between two obstacles, the road between their hit boxes, as drawn, is at least the tallest pack's height plus the one or two lane changes it makes there at a Muddy pack's speed, about a third of the slowest pack's, plus about half a second to react. Off the path, obstacles may stand closer. Two obstacles 3 lanes wide never sit in neighbouring rows: at least one empty row separates them. Narrower ones may, wherever the gap rule and the 2 open lanes per row still hold
- **Mover limits:** at most ten movers are on screen at once (at most six darting and four in-lane movers slower than the road); squirrels and the friendly neighbour block no lane and count only among themselves, against the same limits
- **Visible kind limits:** each hazardous obstacle kind has at most two visible logical instances, fixed and moving together; skins and directions share the same kind limit. Visibility includes entry cues and conservatively allows for post-hit stopped scenery until the last visible part leaves. Each three-bird duckling trail, its leader and two followers, counts as one logical obstacle, so two trails may be visible together. While another instance of the same kind is visible, an incoming instance uses a different leftmost logical spawn lane and entry row, with a seeded entry stagger of at least one walking step. The entry row is the entry scroll distance divided by row spacing, rounded down. These separation rules apply at spawn only; later movement paths may cross naturally
- **Mover placement:**
  - **Crossers:** the planner probes stops and alternative full profiles for compatibility with the open path. The certified layout sets each crosser's stop and profile
  - **Darts:** a dart may set off from a spot something else already holds, crossing over or past it, so a fixed obstacle may stand, and an in-lane mover may pass, where only a dart will cross. Its slot reserves its full swept path before a compatible kind is selected. A dart that blocks a lane (any dart but a squirrel) never blocks the path while it meets the pack, and obstacles may stand on the row before its crossing like any other
  - **Lanes:** two in-lane movers may share a lane when they never meet, always at least a cell's length apart
  - **No room:** for each slot, the planner may try compatible kinds and full movement profiles. The runtime witness is the certified final joint layout, complete or partial: it covers the mandatory slots and the optional requests it keeps, including auxiliary pickups, lucky finds and gear, and preserves the path, full profiles, quiet coverage and safety limits. No reservation is made ahead of each placement for every optional request still sought. Frozen movement and random draws are never redrawn to fix a placement, and actual weighted selection and job order semantics are unchanged within each attempt. Placement preserves the path, open lanes, screen caps and reaction gaps, including reactive lateral reach. Lateral behaviour is not removed to increase counts, and signature paths are never replaced with ordinary movement. Capacity slots are not part of this runtime witness
- **Path:** each neighbourhood draws its path first: it holds each lane for at least 2 rows, its lane stays open in every row, and every lane it crosses to change lanes stays clear in the row before the change. It carries on from the last neighbourhood's lane, and a walk starts it in the pack's lane. Before slot construction, one bounded reconstruction may adjust the path's lanes and holds to accommodate the guarantees' full frozen movement profiles, preserving the starting lane, two-row holds, reachability, two open lanes and obstacle budgets; the final plan still requires independent audit. Wherever lane-blocking spawns wall off a stretch of road in a row, that stretch keeps a lane no dart sweeps, so a pack that dodged into it is never trapped
- **Quiet stretches:** a quiet stretch from a neighbourhood's first row or to its last runs at most half the 3-row limit, rounded down (1 row), so two neighbourhoods' quiet ends never add up past it; a neighbourhood whose rows start in view (the walk's first) has none at its start. Visible obstacle coverage is validated in the certified layout, preserving mover paths, the drawn path, open lanes and reaction gaps
